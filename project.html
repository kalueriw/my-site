Five things I might build

One idea is not a choice — it is a decision already made for you. Five is enough to compare: each one names the real person it is for, and says what would make it worth building.

5 ideas · 1 build order

The ideas

Order does not matter

  • Campus map

    A map where you can search a building by the room number on your timetable and see the shortest door-to-door route, indoors.

  • Ingredient-fusion recipes

    Type in the three things left in your fridge; get a recipe that actually uses them, with a substitution for whatever you do not have.

  • Browser mini-game

    A five-minute game that loads in one page and plays with the arrow keys — no download, no account, no tutorial.

  • Campus forum

    One place to ask about a course, a room or a club — and get an answer from a student who was there last year.

  • Pet chooser

    Answer six questions about your room, your hours and your budget; get three animals that would actually suit that life — and one that would not.

Audience

Who each one is for

“Everyone” is not a person. Each answer below names a group small enough to picture.

Campus map
First-year students in their first week, who can name a building and still cannot find it.
Ingredient-fusion recipes
Students cooking alone in a dorm kitchen: three ingredients, one hob, no car.
Browser mini-game
Someone with five minutes between lectures, on a lab computer where nothing can be installed.
Campus forum
A first-year with a question at 11 p.m. and no one awake to ask.
Pet chooser
A first-time owner who has already chosen the animal and is only now checking what it needs.

Build order

How I would build the campus map

Step 3 before step 1 is not a different style. It is a different project.

  1. Walk the route myself, twice, and write down the room numbers and the doors on paper.
  2. Write the HTML: a list of buildings, a list of rooms, and one search box.
  3. Style it so the map reads on a phone held in one hand, outside.
  4. Make the search work, then make it work with the keyboard.
  5. Give it to ten first-years and watch where they get lost.
