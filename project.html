Shortlist, not wish list

Five things I might build

One idea is not a choice — it is a decision you have already made. Five is enough to compare them: each one names a real person it is for, and says what would make it worth the work.

The ideas

  • Campus map

    A map where you can search a building by the room number on your timetable and see the shortest door-to-door route, indoors.

  • Ingredient-fusion recipes

    Type in the three things left in your fridge; get a recipe that actually uses them, with a substitution for whatever you do not have.

  • Photo portfolio

    A calendar of photographs with nothing on the page except the picture, the place, and the date.

  • Band page

    One page for a local band: the next show, the last recording, and a way to book them without a DM.

  • Hometown food

    A guide to what to order in my hometown and what each dish is actually like, written for someone who has never eaten it.

Who each one is for

Campus map
First-year students in their first week, who can name a building and still cannot find it.
Ingredient-fusion recipes
Students cooking alone in a dorm kitchen: three ingredients, one hob, no car.
Photo portfolio
The one person who screens applications, who will give it ninety seconds before deciding.
Band page
Someone who heard the band at a show last month and wants to know when the next one is.
Hometown food
Classmates from other provinces who have never seen this food and do not know what to order.

How I would build the campus map

  1. Walk the route myself, twice, and write down the room numbers and the doors on paper.
  2. Write the HTML: a list of buildings, a list of rooms, and one search box.
  3. Style it so the map reads on a phone held in one hand, outside.
  4. Make the search work, then make it work with the keyboard.
  5. Give it to ten first-years and watch where they get lost.
