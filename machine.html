    The idea

    Why this is a useless machine

    A machine with one job — giving your money back — and no way to do anything else.

    In 1950 Claude Shannon built a box with one switch and a hand inside that flipped the switch back. In 1952 the mathematician Marvin Minsky described a machine whose only job was to switch itself off. The joke is the same: you do something, the machine undoes it, and nothing changes.

    This page is that joke at a vending machine. Every shelf is empty. You feed a coin, the machine checks, finds nothing to sell you, and returns the coin. You cannot buy anything and the machine cannot keep anything. The score is how many times it has handed your money back in sixty seconds.

    It has the four parts a useless machine needs. Remove one and it becomes something else:

    Simple
    Twelve coins and one return slot. There is nothing to buy, nothing to choose, and no way to read the label wrong.
    Unexpected
    It does not return the coin you are watching. It returns one you just fed, with a small brass arm, and looks pleased about it.
    Back to the start
    When the run ends, every coin is back in the tray and the machine is empty. Start again and nothing has changed.
    Funny, then not
    The first run is a gag. By the fifth it is a machine with a small amount of power — the power to refuse you — and it will not give it up.
